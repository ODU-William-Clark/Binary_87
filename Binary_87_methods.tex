\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We reproduce the binary-galaxy mass-to-light analysis of Schweizer (1987,
ApJS 64, 427; hereafter S87, with Papers I and II at ApJS 64, 411 and 417).
This note is written so the result can be regenerated from scratch.
Section~\ref{sec:method} summarises the method, Section~\ref{sec:repro} gives
the exact recipe, and Section~\ref{sec:dev} lists every point at which we
depart from S87, classified as either a deliberate \emph{improvement} or a
\emph{liberty} taken where the paper does not specify enough detail. Code:
\url{https://github.com/ODU-William-Clark/Binary_87}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

For a Keplerian pair of total mass $M$ at separation $r$ with eccentricity $e$
and eccentric anomaly $E$, $v^2 = GM(1+e\cos E)/r$, so the quantity
\begin{equation}
\Psi \;\equiv\; \frac{|\Delta v|}{\sqrt{M}}
\;=\; \left[\frac{G\,(1+e\cos E)}{r}\right]^{1/2}
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
carries no dependence on the mass. S87 builds the distribution of the projected
$\Psi_p$ by Monte Carlo, conditional on projected separation $r_p$, then asks
for which trial \ML{} the observed sample is consistent with being drawn from
it.

Writing $M_i = y\,[\,q(t_1)L_1 + q(t_2)L_2\,]_i$ for pair $i$, where $q(t)$ is a
morphology-dependent \ML{} shape and $y$ the scaling being fitted, one forms
\begin{equation}
u_i \;=\; \Phi\!\left(\Psi_{\mathrm{obs},i} \,\middle|\, r_{p,i},\ \mathrm{model}\right),
\qquad
\Psi_{\mathrm{obs},i} = \frac{\Delta v_i}{\sqrt{M_i}} ,
\end{equation}
with $\Phi$ the cumulative conditional distribution, convolved with a Gaussian
of width $\sigma_{v,i}/\sqrt{M_i}$ to carry the velocity errors. If $y$ is
correct the $u_i$ are uniform on $[0,1]$. Uniformity is tested with a $\chi^2$
over five equal cells, accumulating the bound-pair probabilities $P(B|C_i)$
rather than integer counts, and $y$ is scanned to minimise $\chi^2$.

The five eccentricity distributions are $f_1(e)=\delta(0)$, $f_2=2(1-e)$,
$f_3=1$, $f_4=2e$, $f_5=\delta(0.9)$ (S87 eqs.~43a--43e), and the three
morphology functions are $q_1(t)=6.47-0.39t$, $q_2(t)=2$ for $t\le0$ and $1$
otherwise, and $q_3(t)=1$ (eqs.~46a--46c).

\section{Reproduction recipe}
\label{sec:repro}

\paragraph{Data.} 48 southern pairs (96 galaxies) with radial velocities from
Paper I and $K$-magnitudes and $B\!-\!V$ from Paper II, tabulated in
\texttt{Binary\_gal\_87.txt}. After the $P(B|C)>0.5$ cut, $N=43$ pairs enter
the fit, matching S87.

\paragraph{Environment.} Python 3 with \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{scipy},
\texttt{pandas}, \texttt{matplotlib}, \texttt{astropy}.

\paragraph{Order of execution.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_luminosity\_calculator.py} --- B1950 coordinates to
        galactic, correction to the Local Group standard of rest (apex
        $V_\odot = 308$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, $l=105^\circ$, $b=-7^\circ$; Yahil
        et al.\ 1977), then $L$, $r_p$ and $v_p$ per pair.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_probabilites.py} --- simulates fields of optical
        (chance) pairs and returns $P(B|C)$ per velocity bin via Bayes'
        theorem.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_r\_distro.py} --- Lucy deconvolution of the
        lognormal $f(r_p)=\exp[-0.63(\ln r_p - 4.32)^2]$ (S87 eq.~39) into the
        spatial distribution $f(r)$.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_Para\_Main\_2.py} --- Monte Carlo ensembles
        (S87 steps M1--M8), writing \texttt{simulated\_psi\_data\_test.npz}.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_ML\_8.py} --- the $y$ scan, writing
        \texttt{ML\_fit\_results.csv}.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_Chi\_Square\_3.py} --- best-fit $y$ per model.
  \item \texttt{Binary\_87\_calibrate\_chi2.py} --- parametric bootstrap of the
        minimised $\chi^2$ (see \S\ref{sec:chi2}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Configuration used.} \texttt{n\_samples} $=5\times10^5$;
\texttt{nb} $=5$ cells; \texttt{NBINS\_PSI} $=50$;
\texttt{USE\_R\_CONDITIONING} $=$ True with \texttt{N\_R\_BINS} $=8$ quantile
bins; \texttt{PSI\_RANGE\_PCT} $=$ None (upper histogram edge at
$1.2\max\Psi_p$); $y$ grid of 1000 linear points on $[0.1,500]$;
$H_0 = 50$\,km\,s$^{-1}$\,Mpc$^{-1}$; $r \in [10,1500]$\,kpc;
\texttt{APPLY\_MIN\_SEP} $=$ False for fidelity to S87
(Table~\ref{tab:dev}). Runtimes: step 2 $\approx 9$\,s, step 4 $\approx 7$\,s,
step 5 $\approx 35$\,s.

\section{Corrections to our own implementation}

These are not departures from S87 --- they are defects in our code that were
returning us \emph{to} her method. They are recorded because they change the
answer materially.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{Projection geometry.} S87 step M7 projects $r$ onto the plane
        of the sky and $\Psi$ onto the line of sight using a single random
        orientation. We had been drawing two independent angles, uniform in
        angle rather than uniform on the sphere. Validation of the corrected
        code: $\langle r_p/r \rangle = 0.7856$ (exact $\pi/4 = 0.7854$),
        $\langle (r_p/r)^2 \rangle = 0.6669$ (exact $2/3$),
        $\langle (\Psi_p/\Psi)^2 \rangle = 0.3334$ (exact $1/3$); the previous
        version gave $0.637$, $0.500$ and $0.500$. At fixed $r$ the true
        correlation is $\mathrm{corr}(r_p,\Psi_p) = +0.17$, where the old code
        gave $-0.0008$. Best-fit $y$ rose by a median factor $1.84$.
  \item \textbf{Conditional rather than marginal PDF.} S87 step M8 histograms
        the ensemble jointly in $r_p$ and $\Psi_p$, and step 7 of her inverse
        transformation evaluates $\Phi(\Psi_p \,|\, r_p, \mathrm{model})$. We
        had been using the marginal $\Psi_p$ distribution.
  \item \textbf{Sampled $r$ was overwritten} by an independent redraw after
        projection, decoupling the stored $r$ from the stored $\Psi$.
  \item \textbf{Velocities in the optical-pair simulation} were drawn once per
        \emph{pair}, so a galaxy received independent velocities for every
        partner it was paired with. Now drawn once per galaxy.
  \item \textbf{Polynomial window.} S87 \S VI(b)iii fits a second-order
        polynomial over all points with $\chi^2 \le 3\chi^2_{\min}$. We had
        briefly narrowed this to $\Delta\chi^2 \le 1$; restored.
\end{enumerate}

Degenerate polynomial fits &
Fall back to the raw $\arg\min$ of the scan when the fitted parabola has
$a \le 0$ or its vertex lies off the grid. S87 reports her $\chi^2(y)$ curves
as ``nearly parabolic'' and does not discuss failures; ours are not, and the
unguarded fit returned a \emph{negative} \ML{} for $f_1$. &
\textbf{Improvement} \\[4pt]

Uncertainty on $y$ &
We report the $\Delta\chi^2 = 1$ interval per model, in addition to her
polynomial-fit 95\% interval. S87 instead adopts a single 22\% formal error
for all solutions, taken from her closure test (\S VI[c]). &
\textbf{Improvement} \\[4pt]

Interpretation of $\chi^2_\nu$ &
We calibrate the \emph{minimised} $\chi^2$ by parametric bootstrap rather than
comparing it against unity (\S\ref{sec:chi2}). &
\textbf{Improvement} \\[4pt]

Kepler solver &
Vectorised Newton--Raphson (residual $8.9\times10^{-16}$) in place of a generic
root finder. Numerical only; no change to the science. &
\textbf{Improvement} \\[4pt]

Minimum-separation cut &
S87 bias B5 applies \emph{no} correction for pair overlap. Our code retains an
optional cut (\texttt{APPLY\_MIN\_SEP}) whose functional form we could not
source; it is inert at these separations and should be left off. &
\textbf{Liberty} (unused) \\[4pt]

Monte Carlo ensemble size &
$5\times10^5$ per model. S87 does not state hers; with 8 $r_p$ bins, $10^4$
samples leaves only ${\sim}23$ per $\Psi_p$ bin. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

$\Psi_p$ histogram binning &
50 bins, upper edge at $1.2\max\Psi_p$. Unspecified in S87. Note this edge
drifts with \texttt{n\_samples}; a high percentile
(\texttt{PSI\_RANGE\_PCT}) is available and is statistically safer, though it
did not improve agreement with her Table~4. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

$r_p$ conditioning resolution &
8 quantile bins in $r_p$. S87 states only that the ensemble is ``histogrammed
in $r_p$ and $\Psi_p$''; the grid is not given. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

$y$ grid &
1000 linear points on $[0.1,500]$. S87 gives the range but not the sampling.
The step of $0.5$ is coarse for $q_1$, where $y \sim 5$. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

Lucy deconvolution &
10 iterations on a $10^4$-point grid. S87 Fig.~15 displays the \emph{fifth}
iterative approximation, so she may have used 5. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

M8 rejection function &
$P_{\mathrm{reject}}(r_p) = \exp(r_p/255)\,/\,\exp(800/255)$, i.e.\ everything
wider than 800\,kpc removed. We could not verify these constants against her
eq.~(26). &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

Optical-pair field model &
50--150 galaxies per $10^\circ \times 10^\circ$ field, magnitudes uniform on
$[9,18]$, positions Poisson. Real galaxies are clustered, so the chance-pair
rate is underestimated and $P(B|C)$ is biased high. &
\textbf{Liberty} \\[4pt]

Fallback velocity error &
$\sigma_v = 9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ where multiple spectrograms are unavailable. S87
says ``the mean sample velocity error''; Paper I quotes
$\langle \sigma_v \rangle \approx 9$\,km\,s$^{-1}$. &
\textbf{Liberty} (well grounded) \\
\end{longtable}

\section{Reading the \texorpdfstring{$\chi^2$}{chi-squared}}
\label{sec:chi2}

The quantity reported is the \emph{minimum} of $\chi^2$ over the $y$ scan. A
minimum is biased low and must not be compared against the degrees of freedom.
A parametric bootstrap ($f_4/q_3$, 400 synthetic samples drawn from the model
at its own best-fit $y$ and pushed through the identical scan) gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
unminimised null, mean & 3.88 \quad ($\simeq \nu = 4$) \\
minimised $\chi^2$, mean & 2.03 \\
minimised $\chi^2$, median & 1.55 \\
\textbf{implied ``perfect fit'' $\chi^2_\nu$} & \textbf{0.51 (mean), 0.39 (median)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

S87's own Table~4 values run $0.53$--$1.02$, and she selects the model with the
\emph{lowest} $\chi^2_\nu$ ($f_4$, at $0.53$), not the one nearest unity. Her
remark that ``in $\chi^2$ tests with a good fit, $\chi^2_\nu$ should ideally be
approximately equal to unity'' is the textbook ideal, not what the analysis
achieves. Binning must not be tuned to reach it.

We follow S87 in taking $\nu = 5 - 1 = 4$ (five cells, one restriction, the
number of pairs being fixed); she does not charge a degree of freedom for $y$.

Her published result is $\ML_V = 21 \pm 5$ for Sc and $39 \pm 9$ for E, with

these on an $H_0 = 70$ scale.

\section{Open issues}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
  \item \textbf{The $\chi^2(y)$ curve is jagged.} $\chi^2$ steps whenever a
        $u_i$ crosses one of the five cell boundaries, producing many local
        minima within $\Delta\chi^2 < 2$. The global $\arg\min$ is therefore
        unstable, and S87's values fall on \emph{some} of our local minima but
        not always the lowest. Percentile histogram ranges (98--99.9),
        Savitzky--Golay smoothing of $\chi^2(y)$, and coarsening the $y$ grid
        all failed to stabilise it. The principled fix is a statistic
        continuous in $y$ --- Cram\'er--von Mises or Anderson--Darling on the
        $u_i$, or a direct likelihood
        $\sum_i \log f(\Psi_i \,|\, r_{p,i}, y)$ --- which removes the
        jaggedness at source and yields proper confidence intervals. That
        would be a departure from S87 justified as an improvement, and is the
        recommended next step.
  \item \textbf{The closure test has not been run.} S87 \S VI(c) generates 10
        synthetic samples of 43 bound pairs on circular orbits with known
        \ML{} and recovers
        $(\ML)_{\mathrm{out}}/(\ML)_{\mathrm{in}} = 1.03 \pm 0.07$, from which
        her 22\% formal error follows. Repeating it here is the only clean way
        to decide whether the residual Table~4 differences are bias or noise.
\end{enumerate}
